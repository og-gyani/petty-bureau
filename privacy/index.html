Privacy Policy

This policy covers the Petty Bureau iPhone app and its widgets.

Short version  Petty Bureau doesn't collect any data. What you put in it stays on your iPhone.

What we collect

Nothing. Petty Bureau has no accounts, no servers, no analytics, no ads and no third-party code. We never receive what you type, your photos, your location, your reminders, your Health or Screen Time data, or anything else you do in the app. We don't sell or share your data, because we never have it.

Where your things are kept

Petty Bureau saves your entries on your iPhone, in the app's own storage and in a shared space that only Petty Bureau and its widgets can open. Nothing is uploaded.

Widgets, Live Activities and notifications can show your entries on your Home Screen and Lock Screen. Your iPhone's notification and widget settings control what shows when the phone is locked.

If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or a computer, Petty Bureau's data is part of that backup. Apple or your computer handles the backup, not us.

Permissions

Petty Bureau asks for each permission when you first use the part that needs it. You can say no, and the rest of the app still works. You can change your answer any time in Settings > Apps > Petty Bureau.

  • Notifications: for verdict days, evictions, check-ins and the other moments you set up. They are scheduled on your iPhone, not sent from a server.
  • Camera (Proof I Locked The Door): to take proof photos. The photos stay on your iPhone.
  • Location while using the app (Proof I Locked The Door): to show how far a photo was taken from a spot you saved. Petty Bureau never looks up street addresses.
  • Microphone (Crashout Jar): only while you hold the button, to measure how loud you are. Petty Bureau keeps the loudness numbers and never records or saves audio.
  • Reminders (To-Do Obituaries): to find reminders nobody has touched in a while. Petty Bureau changes a reminder only when you ask it to, for example to check one off or give it a new due date.
  • Photos, add only: to save the cards you choose to keep.
  • Photo library (Camera Roll Roast): to count habits in your library, like screenshots, bursts and 2 AM photos. The counting happens on your iPhone. Your photos never leave it, and the roast shows numbers, never your photos.
  • Health, read only (Aura Score, Receipt of Your Day): your steps, sleep and time in daylight. Petty Bureau never writes to Health, and the numbers stay on your iPhone unless you share a card.
  • Screen Time (Gremlin): how long you spend in the apps you pick, read through Apple's Screen Time framework. Petty Bureau can't see which apps you picked, only the minutes. They are used on your iPhone only and never collected or sent.

On-device AI

On iPhones that have it, Petty Bureau uses Apple's on-device language model to give an entry a short nickname, like "the Friday text". The model runs on your iPhone. Your words are not sent to us or to any AI service. The jokes are fixed lines written ahead of time and built into the app; the model only picks a short name for your entry.

Sharing

Share cards are images Petty Bureau draws on your iPhone. They leave the app only when you tap Share and choose where they go. After that, the app you shared to handles them under its own privacy policy.

What Apple shares with us

If you turned on Share With App Developers (Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements), Apple may share crash reports and app usage statistics with us. Apple collects those under Apple's privacy policy. We don't collect anything ourselves.

Children

Petty Bureau collects no data from anyone, including children.

Deleting your data

  • Delete the Petty Bureau app to remove everything it stored on your iPhone. Some toys also let you delete a single entry.
  • Turning off a permission in Settings stops Petty Bureau from using it. Anything already saved stays until you delete it or the app.
  • We hold no copy of your data, so there is nothing to ask us to delete.

Changes

If Petty Bureau ever starts collecting data, we will update this policy and the App Store privacy label before that version is released. The date at the top shows the latest change.
